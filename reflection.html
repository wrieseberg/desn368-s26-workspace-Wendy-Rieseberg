Reflection

At the beginning of this course, I knew absolutely nothing about coding. I had no real understanding of how websites were built or what HTML and CSS even involved. My initial reaction was honestly, "oh hell no." Looking at code for the first time felt overwhelming, and it was difficult to imagine ever being able to create a website on my own.

Unlike some learning experiences, there was not one specific moment where everything suddenly clicked. Instead, understanding came through repetition and persistence. There were many times when I would spend hours trying to solve a problem on my own without success. Eventually, I would seek help through tutorials, outside resources, AI tools, or my instructor. Once I saw the solution and understood why it worked, the concept often felt surprisingly logical. Over time, those small moments of understanding began to build on each other.

One of my biggest challenges was creating the Tarot Field Guide. It was the first project that required responsive design, and it pushed me well beyond my comfort zone. At times the project felt overwhelming, and there were moments when I genuinely questioned whether I would be able to complete it. Through a combination of trial and error, persistence, and help from Travis when my code broke, I was able to work through the challenges. Looking back, that project became an important turning point because many of the concepts that felt impossible at the time became much easier in later assignments.

Throughout the quarter, I found that my biggest obstacle was not always coding itself. Often, it was making design decisions. When given complete creative freedom, I sometimes struggled with indecision because there were so many possible directions to explore. Learning to make decisions, commit to a direction, and keep moving forward became an important part of the design process.

AI also played an important role in my learning. Rather than using it to complete assignments for me, I used it as a learning tool. When I encountered a problem, I would often take screenshots, explain what I was trying to accomplish, and ask questions about specific pieces of code. If I did not understand the answer, I continued asking questions until I understood not only what the code was doing, but why it worked. This process helped me build a stronger understanding of HTML and CSS while encouraging me to think critically about the solutions I was implementing.

One area where I saw significant growth was my confidence with GitHub. This was my second quarter using it, and after a great deal of trial and error, the process finally began to make sense. What once felt confusing and intimidating now feels like a useful tool that I can navigate with confidence.

By the end of the quarter, my perspective on coding had changed dramatically. While there are still concepts I continue to learn, especially Flexbox and media queries, coding no longer feels impossible. In fact, I genuinely enjoy it. Without the pressure of deadlines and multiple classes competing for attention, I think building websites would be a lot of fun. I can now see myself continuing to create websites in the future and would even consider design roles that include front-end development responsibilities.

The most important lesson I learned is that growth comes through persistence. When a problem feels overwhelming, the best solution is often to step away, regroup, and return with a fresh perspective. Improvement rarely happens all at once. It happens through practice, repetition, problem solving, and a willingness to keep trying.

At the start of this course, I could not imagine building a responsive website from scratch. Now, after completing multiple projects and developing a portfolio of my work, I understand that if you can design it, you can code it.
